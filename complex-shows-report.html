The short version

The choreography engine is not what limits this show. Two numbers do: a separation margin that is the wrong shape, and a 31-piece firmware ceiling that cannot be lifted the obvious way because of how the show reuses its figures. Fix the first and the arena roughly triples in usable density. Understand the second and you stop planning a rewrite that the firmware would reject.

Everything below is restricted to what runs on the rig as configured. Where a technique needs hardware you do not have, or a port you have not done, that is stated rather than glossed.

Finding 01 — the unlock

Your separation margin is a sphere. The real constraint is an ellipsoid.

safety.py enforces one scalar: MIN_SEPARATION = 0.8 m, planned at 0.90 m after the measured TRACKING_MARGIN. That is isotropic — it treats a drone passing beside another exactly like one passing beneath it. Downwash does not work that way, and the difference was measured on this exact airframe.

Preiss, Hönig et al. flew two Crazyflies directly above one another, logging position error at 100 Hz across separations, and fitted an axis-aligned ellipsoid: rz = 0.30 m vertically, rx = ry = 0.12 m horizontally. The pairwise safety test becomes the closed form ‖E−1(p − q)‖₂ ≥ 2 with E = diag(rx, ry, rz) — i.e. 0.60 m centre-to-centre vertically, but only 0.24 m horizontally. Bitcraze's own firmware agrees on the shape: the stock collision-avoidance module defaults to a tall ellipsoid of 0.3 / 0.3 / 0.9 m.

3.75× How much more horizontal clearance the current isotropic margin demands than the measured downwash requirement — on a show that flies almost entirely planar at form_height = 1.00 m, so every pair is a horizontal pair.

This is why scale below 0.99 is rejected: the show is pressed against a budget that is mostly imaginary in the axis it actually uses. Replacing the scalar test in transition_min_sep and sample_min_sep with the ellipsoidal norm is a contained change to one module, and it stays a pure offline check — plan_show keeps refusing rather than warning.

Finding 02 — the trap

Compression would buy you pieces, and break the show's tempo.

The 31-piece cap is real and exactly as documented: crtp_commander_high_level.c allocates TRAJECTORY_MEMORY_SIZE 4096 with the comment "4k allows us to store 31 poly4d pieces", because one uncompressed piece is 8 float coefficients × 4 axes + 1 duration float = 132 bytes. You use 28.

The obvious relief is the compressed format — 2-byte millimetre integers, per-axis Bézier degree selectable down to constant — which fits far more segments in the same 4 KB. It would break this show. The firmware rejects a compressed trajectory started with any timescale other than 1.0, or with reversed set, returning ENOEXEC. Your tempo column runs figures at ×1.15, ×0.90 and ×0.80, and the reversed carousel is a reversed playback of an already-uploaded figure. Compression and the reuse strategy are mutually exclusive.

The compatible way to escape 31 pieces is to re-upload mid-show: trajectories are written to a chosen start_addr in trajectory memory and selected by id, so figures can be swapped between phases rather than all held resident. That trades piece count against radio activity at a known moment in the timeline — schedulable, and verifiable on the ground.

01 · Richer choreography

Adopt the ellipsoidal separation test

Offline-verifiable

Swap the scalar distance in the two separation routines for ‖E−1(p − q)‖₂ ≥ 2. Keep TRACKING_MARGIN — the measured 0.057 m is controller lag, additive to whatever geometry you use, and the counterflow asymmetry that produced it is unchanged. Inflate the ellipsoid radii by the margin rather than adding it to a scalar.

What it takes
One change in safety.py; the closed-form quadratic minimisation in transition_min_sep still works, in the E-scaled coordinates.
Live-show risk
Low, but it removes conservatism you have been flying behind. Re-measure lag on hardware before spending all of it.
plan_show
Unchanged contract — still a pure geometric predicate, still refuses.

Use the vertical axis you are not using

Offline-verifiable

The show is planar at 1.00 m apart from the bloom's 0.45 m rise. Under the ellipsoid the axes trade against each other: horizontal packing gets much cheaper, vertical stacking gets more expensive than your current margin assumes (0.60 m required, and downwash is the reason). That asymmetry is itself choreographic material — tight interleaved rings that would be illegal as a stack, staggered-altitude waves where the vertical offsets are deliberately larger than the horizontal ones.

What it takes
New figures in figures.py with a z-component; the 360° composition rule and piece budget apply unchanged.
Live-show risk
Real. Vertical proximity is where downwash actually bites, and where a lagging drone is least recoverable.
plan_show
Already samples 3-D positions at 200 Hz; the anisotropic test covers it directly.

Optimise assignment for makespan, not total distance

Offline-verifiable

assign_min_distance brute-forces the permutation minimising summed travel — the Hungarian objective, and at n=5 the 120 permutations are free. But a formation change is simultaneous: what you care about is when the last drone arrives, which is the linear bottleneck assignment problem, not sum-of-costs. Hönig et al. make exactly this distinction and use the Threshold algorithm before planning. Changing the key from a sum to a max is a one-line edit that shortens every gather leg to its true critical path.

What it takes
Change the key in assign_min_distance to max(...); scaled_duration already scales to the longest path, so the two now agree.
Live-show risk
None beyond the figures it enables — shorter legs mean the same distance in less time, so re-check the speed budget.
plan_show
Fully covered; assignment is chosen on the ground and baked into the plan.

Certify corridors with Bézier control points instead of sampling

Offline-verifiable

Your 200 Hz sampling note is honest about its own weakness: a grid can step over a brief close approach. There is a stronger guarantee available. A polynomial in the Bernstein basis is guaranteed to lie within the convex hull of its control points, so constraining control points to a safe polyhedron proves containment without sampling at all — the property the T-RO 2018 planner is built on, at the same degree-7 / C⁴ form the Crazyflie already executes. figures.py has bezier_to_poly already.

What it takes
Meaningful work: express figures in control points, define per-drone corridors. Worth it if figures get dense enough that sampling confidence drops.
Live-show risk
None added — it is a strictly stronger pre-flight proof than the current one.
plan_show
Upgrades it from "checked at 200 Hz" to "proved". Keep the sampler as a cross-check.

02 · New capabilities

Light choreography — needs five LED-ring decks first

Hardware, then verifiable

This is the one item on the list that is not buildable as the rig stands: the LED ring is an expansion deck, 3.3 g and up to 1 A per drone, not part of a stock CF 2.x. With decks fitted it is genuinely easy — the firmware exposes a whole ring parameter group over the normal Crazyswarm2 param path, no custom firmware:

ParameterDoes whatNotes for a timed show
ring.effectSelects one of 19 effects by id7 = solid colour, 13 = per-LED memory, 14 = fade, 17 = onboard timeline
ring.solidRed/Green/BlueRGB for effect 7uint8 each; three writes per cue
ring.fadeColorTarget colour, packed 0x00RRGGBBWith ring.fadeTime in seconds; fadeTime 0 = instant
deck.bcLedRingRead-only deck presenceUse it as a pre-flight check that all five decks enumerated

Two ceilings matter. The ring is repainted by a 50 ms FreeRTOS timer — 20 Hz, so no sub-50 ms strobe exists regardless of how precisely ROS 2 schedules the write. And effect 17 plays a colour timeline from onboard memory against the drone's own clock, which is the right mechanism for beat-sync because it is immune to radio jitter — but the buffer holds only LEDRING_TIME_MEM_SIZE = 10 entries, each a duration in 1/25 s units. Ten cues per drone, then you are back on the radio.

What it takes
Buy and fit 5 decks. Then setParam cues, or a 24-byte RGB565 frame per drone via MEM_TYPE_LED12.
Live-show risk
Payload and current draw cut endurance; and see the controller-mass warning below — added deck mass mistracks Mellinger.
plan_show
Can validate every cue against the legal effect-id set and the 10-entry timeline cap. Colour needs no trajectory check.

Music synchronisation — you have already built the mechanism

Offline-verifiable

The show is a timed plan with a per-figure timescale column. Beat-sync is not new machinery; it is choosing those timescales so figure boundaries land on bars, which build_plan already composes and plan_show already totals. Because every figure returns each drone to its starting angle, figures retime independently without disturbing the geometry — the composition rule pays off exactly here.

What it takes
Solve timescales against a bar grid instead of hand-tuning. Speed and acceleration scale with 1/timescale², so the budget check does the policing.
Live-show risk
Low. The risk is musical, not mechanical: audio playback and the flight node share no clock, so cue the music from the show, not the reverse.
plan_show
Already covers it — retiming is what the existing budgets are for.

Shape and text formations — five drones is five points

Offline-verifiable

Worth stating plainly to avoid a wasted effort: the canonical text-forming demo (the "USC" letters) used 32 Crazyflies. With five you are placing five points, so the honest target is recognisable constellations and silhouettes — an arrow, a chevron, a rotating cross — not glyphs. ngon_slots, line_slots and grid_slots already give you the primitive; what is missing is a slot-set-to-slot-set transition planner, which is Findings 01 and 03 above.

What it takes
A shape → slot list function plus the makespan assignment; both are small next to what exists.
Live-show risk
Low — these are goTo legs, the case transition_min_sep solves exactly rather than by sampling.
plan_show
Fully covered, including the phase sweep in best_phase_ngon.

03 · Scale and robustness

Radio is not your constraint. Bitcraze put ~15 Crazyflies on one radio while broadcasting pose at 100 Hz; a Crazyswarm2 maintainer reports 10 on one radio with log topics stripped to status. The hard ceiling is 1000 packets per second in Crazyradio firmware, roughly 1 packet per millisecond. Five drones at 2M is comfortable — but every log topic you add spends the same budget, so treat telemetry as a cost, not a freebie.

The failure modes are where the attention belongs, and they are sharper than they look.

Failure semantics

A lost link is a fall, not a hover. The firmware supervisor arms a keepalive watchdog on the first keepalive packet; thereafter one must arrive at least every 1000 ms or the drone emergency-stops, cuts motors and enters a latching locked state that requires a reboot. Tumble detection behaves the same way — crashed state, motors off, free fall, no recovery attempt. Plan clearance on the assumption that a dropped drone descends immediately and does not stop.

ConcernStock mechanismWhat to do about it
Mid-show abort emergency() / cmdStop() Terminal and latching — ends the flight, needs a power cycle. Make a staged /all/land the default abort and reserve emergency for genuine loss of control.
Silent command refusal EBUSY from HL commander When the supervisor blocks the commander it calls plan_stop() and every subsequent takeoff/goTo/startTrajectory returns EBUSY. If the client ignores the status byte a drone silently stops flying the show. Check it.
Low battery voltage_warning 3.8 / critical 3.7 Declarative per robot_type in crazyflies.yaml. Add pm.vbat as a logging topic only if you accept the radio cost.
Identity swap Motive rigid bodies Motive requires non-congruent marker arrangements; two congruent bodies that come close can swap labels — the wrong pose to the wrong drone, mid-figure. Highest-consequence mocap failure for tight choreography.
Tracking dropout min markers to boot / continue Per-asset thresholds in Motive define when a drone is declared untracked. That is the trigger to wire an abort to.

Consider single-marker tracking — and weigh what it costs you

Trade-off

Crazyswarm2's own recommendation for most experiments is not vendor rigid bodies: it is one marker per drone with librigidbodytracker and default_single_marker, which re-runs optimal task assignment every mocap frame and is therefore robust to exactly the close-proximity label swap above. The cost is direct: yaw becomes unobservable without motion in the xy-plane. Your figures sweep yaw and your composition rule is built on whole multiples of 360°, so this is a real trade, not a free win — but with a shared Motive server whose rigid-body list may be someone else's configuration on any given day, it removes a class of failure you do not control.

What it takes
Config change in the mocap tracking setup; no code. Reversible.
Live-show risk
Shifts risk rather than removing it: fewer identity swaps, weaker yaw. Test with the yaw-heavy figures specifically.
plan_show
Out of scope — this is a tracking-layer property, invisible to the planner.

Verify on the rig before relying on it

Sources conflict on whether your backend broadcasts. Crazyswarm v1 documentation describes group-masked commands reaching a subset "without needing to send more than one radio packet", and a Crazyswarm2 maintainer describes mocap pose going out as one broadcast for all drones. Against that, the Crazyswarm2 backend support table reportedly lists the CFlib backend as "Broadcasts: No", with all/* services implemented as a per-URI unicast loop. You run cflib on hardware. This decides both your bandwidth arithmetic and whether "simultaneous" figure starts are truly simultaneous or serialised across five unicast calls — worth ten minutes with a timestamped log before designing phase-locked sub-formations on top of it.

04 · Autonomy and control

Onboard collision avoidance is one parameter away — and it voids your guarantee

Not offline-verifiable

Buffered Voronoi Cell avoidance ships in stock firmware and turns on with colAv.enable = 1, declaratively in crazyflies.yaml or at runtime. It is decentralised, takes neighbour positions from peer_localization for drones on the same radio channel — exactly your topology — and tracks up to 10 neighbours. No new hardware, no custom firmware.

Then the caveats, all from the module's own source. It rewrites the setpoint between commander and controller and does not smooth the result, so output may be discontinuous or far from the current state. Only the PID controller is confirmed to work with it; high-gain controllers like Mellinger may go unstable. And when the buffered cell is empty it holds position — a drone can simply stop mid-figure. None of that is reproducible in an offline planner.

What it takes
One parameter. Hyperparameters at collision_avoidance.c lines 348–428 may need tuning.
Live-show risk
Substantial and of a different kind: it trades a predicted show for an unpredictable safety net.
plan_show
Broken by it. Use it during hardware bring-up as a net over rehearsals, then fly the verified show without it.

Controller choice couples to two other decisions

Re-measure required

stabilizer.controller selects PID (1) or Mellinger (2) from crazyflies.yaml — a config change, not a rebuild. Mellinger is the minimum-snap controller and tracks uploaded polynomials more tightly, which is the direct lever on your measured 0.057 m tracking lag. Two couplings decide whether you can take it:

  • Mellinger is mass-dependent. It computes thrust from the firmware's platform mass, so fitting LED decks without updating that mass mistracks. PID does not care.
  • Mellinger and BVCA conflict. Only PID is confirmed against onboard avoidance, so "tight polynomial tracking" and "onboard net" are mutually exclusive choices.

Note also that TRACKING_MARGIN is a PID-lag measurement. Switching controllers invalidates it in the safe direction, but it must be re-measured before being spent.

What it takes
A YAML value, then a sim re-fly and a fresh lag measurement from /tf.
Live-show risk
Moderate: higher gains track better and punish bad setpoints harder.
plan_show
Indirect — it verifies against MAX_SPEED/MAX_ACCEL, which are described as the envelope where PID still tracks closely. Revisit them together.

Peer-to-peer: not worth it on this rig

Custom firmware

For completeness, since decentralised control usually starts here. Crazyflie P2P is broadcast-only today (unicast is still in development), capped at 60 bytes per packet, and exposed only as a C API requiring a flashed firmware app — unreachable from crazyflie_py and invisible to plan_show. It also shares the 2.4 GHz channel with CRTP, so with a Crazyradio PA polling continuously you must rate-limit the link. With five drones and a working mocap feed, it buys nothing you do not already have; BVCA already consumes peer positions through the same mechanism without any of the work.

What it takes
Firmware app development and flashing all five drones.
Live-show risk
Contends for airtime with the pose stream your show depends on.
plan_show
Entirely outside it.

Corrections worth carrying

Two claims that survive casual reading and did not survive verification against source.

Correction

uav_trajectories is minimum-jerk, and its limits are soft. It is widely described as minimum-snap; genTrajectory.cpp sets derivative_order::JERK with N = 8, and upstream documentation is explicit that snap-continuity needs N ≥ 10. More consequentially, its --v_max / --a_max are soft penalty terms (use_soft_constraints = true, weight 100, tolerance 0.1), not enforced bounds — a generated trajectory can exceed them. If you ever adopt this toolchain, plan_show must still check the result independently. Which it does: your refuse-don't-warn design is the right one precisely because generators like this one warn at best.

Correction

Group masks are a firmware feature; single-packet delivery may not be. Up to 8 groups exist, and each drone stores its membership in hlCommander.groupmask (the older COMMAND_SET_GROUP_MASK path is deprecated). That much is firmware-verified. The claim that a group command therefore reaches its subset in one radio packet comes from Crazyswarm v1 docs and may not hold for the cflib backend you run — see the verification note in section 03.

Method and confidence

Assembled from 225 extracted claims across 24 sources — 20 primary (Bitcraze firmware and library documentation, firmware source, Crazyswarm/Crazyswarm2 documentation, peer-reviewed papers with public code) and 4 forum threads, weighted accordingly. Claims were put through adversarial verification, but that phase completed only partially: the run exhausted its session budget twice, so a subset of claims carry a full 3-vote check and the rest rest on primary-source quotation alone.
